## 1. Electrical Architecture Overview

The micro wind turbine employs a permanent magnet DC/3-phase AC dynamo driven directly by the horizontal axis rotor. The erratic mechanical rotation generated by turbulent ambient airflow creates a fluctuating, wild AC/pulsed DC voltage output. To reliably power USB mobile devices, IoT sensor nodes, or recharge lithium-ion power banks, the electrical subsystem must perform low-loss rectification, bulk capacitive filtering, and precision buck regulation.

```
 [ 3-Blade Rotor ] 
       │ (Mechanical Shaft Rotation, 2.2 - 14 m/s wind)
       ▼
 [ Coreless Dynamo Generator ]
       │ (Wild AC / Fluctuating Voltage: 1.8V to 18.5V)
       ▼
 [ Schottky Full-Bridge Rectifier ] (4x 1N5819, Ultra-low Vf = 0.32V)
       │ (Pulsating DC)
       ▼
 [ Low-ESR Filter Stage ] (1000 µF / 25V + 100 nF Ceramic High-Freq Decoupling)
       │ (Smooth DC Bus: 3.5V to 16.2V)
       ▼
 [ Synchronous Buck Regulator ] (LM2596 / MP1584, 92% Efficiency)
       │ (Regulated 5.00V ± 0.05V DC)
       ▼
 [ Dual Output: USB-A Port & Terminal Block ] (350 mA - 1.2 A Max Output)
```

## 2. Diode Selection & Conduction Loss Analysis

Traditional silicon full-bridge rectifiers (e.g., 1N4007) exhibit a forward voltage drop ($V_f$) of approximately $0.7\text{V}$ to $1.0\text{V}$ per diode. In a full bridge configuration, current must traverse two diodes in series:

$$V_{drop\_silicon} = 2 \times 0.85\text{V} = 1.70\text{V}$$

At low cut-in wind velocities ($2.2\text{ m/s}$), where the generator produces only $2.5\text{V} - 3.2\text{V}$, standard silicon diodes would dissipate **over 60% of the total generated energy as heat**, completely preventing power harvesting!

### The Schottky Solution (1N5819):
By utilizing **1N5819 Schottky barrier rectifiers**:
- Forward Voltage Drop: $V_f \approx 0.32\text{V} \text{ at } 200\text{ mA}$
- Total Bridge Conduction Drop: $V_{drop\_schottky} = 2 \times 0.32\text{V} = 0.64\text{V}$
- Voltage Recovery Benefit: **+1.06 Volts saved**, lowering cut-in harvesting velocity to **2.2 m/s**.

## 3. Bulk Smoothing Filter Derivation

To maintain a peak-to-peak ripple ($\Delta V_{ripple}$) under $5\%$ on the DC bus before entering the buck switching regulator at maximum rated current ($I_{load} = 0.5\text{A}$) and a lowest turbine frequency of $f = 25\text{ Hz}$ (approx 500 RPM on a 6-pole generator):

$$C_{filter} = \frac{I_{load}}{2 \cdot f \cdot \Delta V_{ripple}} = \frac{0.50\text{ A}}{2 \times 25\text{ Hz} \times 0.30\text{ V}} \approx 333\text{ }\mu\text{F}$$

A standard **$1000\text{ }\mu\text{F} \text{ / } 25\text{V}$ low-ESR electrolytic capacitor** was selected, providing a **$3.0\times$ safety margin** against voltage ripple and transient dips during wind gusts.

## 4. Voltage Regulator Specifications
- **Input Voltage Range:** $4.5\text{V}$ to $28.0\text{V}$ DC
- **Output Voltage:** Fixed $5.00\text{V} \pm 1\%$
- **Switching Frequency:** $1.5\text{ MHz}$ (MP1584) / $150\text{ kHz}$ (LM2596)
- **Quiescent Current:** $< 1.2\text{ mA}$
- **Protection:** Over-current limit, thermal shutdown ($150^\circ\text{C}$), reverse polarity fuse.
